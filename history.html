M30 Collective · The Story So Far

Rebirthing a
Neighborhood

Before the concerts, the courts, and the first residents, there was 46 acres of overlooked ground along the Monon Trail — and a lot of people who believed it could be something more. This is how it got here.

Chapter One

The Ground

For years, the land around East 30th Street and the Monon Trail sat quiet — rail corridor, open lots, and a neighborhood waiting on someone to see what it could be. The trail ran right through it. The city skyline stood right there. What was missing was the belief that this corner of Indianapolis was worth building on.

Chapter Two

The Belief

The plan was first proposed years before the first building opened, and it took patience to get there. What carried it was the people around here — neighbors and believers who held onto the idea that this area deserved a gathering place of its own. When the first building finally opened, the headline told the story plainly:

“Four years after first proposed, Monon 30 has one building open”

Coverage of the district’s long road from proposal to first opening — and of everything still to come.

Chapter Three

Breaking Ground

Belief turned into blueprints, and blueprints into foundations. Concrete pumps on the townhome sites, footings along the trail, survey lines where streets and gathering places now take shape — this is the work that turned a proposal into a place.

Chapter Four

Rising

Then it started to look like the renderings. DOMINO welcomed its first residents in 2025. MONON 30 opened its doors for concerts, dining, and events. And at The Queue, the foundations in these photos are becoming townhomes — the next neighbors, arriving spring 2027.

The story isn’t finished.

A rebirth isn’t a ribbon cutting — it’s what happens when a neighborhood comes back to life, one building, one gathering, one neighbor at a time. M30 Collective is still becoming. Come be part of what’s next.
